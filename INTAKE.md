# Intake

Real-use annoyances, one entry each. This is the queue that feeds design and
planning work — not a bug tracker and not a wishlist. An entry earns its place
by having actually happened to someone using the product.

Write the complaint before the solution. A well-stated annoyance often turns out
to have a different fix than the one its reporter had in mind, and sometimes
turns out to be a behavior that is load-bearing for something else.

## Entry contract

Each entry carries four parts, in this order:

- **Complaint, in the owner's terms.** What happened, what was expected, and why
  the difference mattered. No implementation language and no proposed fix.
- **Chesterton check.** Why the current behavior might exist on purpose. Look
  before concluding it is a mistake — `DESIGN.md`'s doctrine and the dated
  scope records are where the reasoning usually is. An entry that skips this
  step is not ready to act on.
- **Spec implication.** What would have to become true in `DESIGN.md` for the
  complaint to be answered — a doctrine change, a behavior change, a scope
  boundary moving, or nothing at all.
- **Resolution.** Fixed and where, rejected and why, or folded into a named
  plan. An entry stays open until one of those three is true.
